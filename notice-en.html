QUICK START

First Use

  1. Enter an ICAO code (e.g. KJFK) or an airfield name (e.g. New York) in the search bar.
  2. Click "METAR" (current observation) or "TAF" (forecast).
  3. The chart and all widgets draw automatically: dashboard (wind, flight category, GO/NO-GO), flight window, regional map, takeoff performance, frequencies.

Tip: You can also paste a raw METAR or TAF message directly into the editor area. The tool will decode and display it.

Arrival Cursor

Click and drag on the chart to set your planned arrival time (UTC). All TAF forecasts recalculate at that instant. Double-click to reset.

My Aircraft Fleet

You rarely fly a single type of aircraft. Fleet management lets you register your aircraft, each with its own reference distances from the Pilot Operating Handbook (POH):

  • Adding an aircraft: click the "My fleet" button (plane icon, in the takeoff widget). Enter the name, registration, type and ground roll / 50 ft obstacle distances.
  • Autocomplete: as you type the name/model, the tool automatically suggests the most common aircraft (Cessna, Piper, Robin, Diamond, Cirrus…) with their reference distances pre-filled. Always verify against your aircraft's POH.
  • Active aircraft: one aircraft is selected as active for performance calculations. Switch with a single click.
  • Safety margin: each aircraft has its own margin (%) applied to the calculated distances.
  • Persistence: your fleet is stored in your browser.

DECISION SUPPORT

GO / NO-GO Banner

The flagship feature: a banner that crosses all available data and displays a verdict at a glance.

VerdictColorMeaning
GO GreenAll parameters are green — you can fly safely
CAUTION OrangeFlying possible but stay alert — one or more parameters are marginal
NO-GO RedFlight not recommended or prohibited — a critical parameter is exceeded

Criteria automatically evaluated:

  1. Aeronautical night
  2. IFR / LIFR conditions (ceiling and visibility)
  3. Personal minima danger alerts (red) → NO-GO
  4. MVFR conditions → CAUTION
  5. Personal minima warning alerts (orange) → CAUTION
  6. High density altitude (degraded performance)
  7. Crosswind (based on active runway)
  8. Falling QNH trend (imminent deterioration)
  9. Active SIGMET / AIRMET in the area

Transparency: the verdict always lists all reasons. No opaque decisions.

button in the GO / NO-GO header: opens the configuration panel for your personal VFR minima (ceiling, visibility, wind, gusts, temperature, QNH) — see below.

Density Altitude Calculator

Density altitude is the altitude at which the standard atmosphere (ISA) would have the same density as the actual air. This is what determines your aircraft's real performance: climb rate, takeoff distance, landing distance.

Computed from the field elevation, QNH and actual outside air temperature (OAT).

QNH Trend (3 h)

Every METAR contains the QNH (pressure reduced to sea level — the "Q1021" at the end of the message). By comparing the observations of the last 3 hours, the tool shows the trend: steady, rising (likely improvement) or falling.

A fast fall — more than 2 hPa per hour — signals an approaching or deepening low: typically what precedes strengthening wind, lowering ceilings and precipitation. This reason then appears in the GO / NO-GO banner.

Example: 14 h Q1018 → 15 h Q1015 → 16 h Q1011 = −7 hPa in 2 h (≈ 3.5 hPa/h): "fast fall" alert. A valuable clue, not a certainty — the decision remains the pilot's.

Takeoff Performance Widget

Estimates your corrected takeoff distances (ground roll + 50 ft obstacle) based on density altitude, surface type and your active aircraft. Compares the result to the available runway length with a visual margin bar.

  • Runway length detected automatically (editable manually).
  • Applies your personal safety margin.
  • Alert if the calculated distance exceeds the available runway.

Personal VFR Minima

The button in the GO / NO-GO header opens the configuration of your personal thresholds:

  • Ceiling, visibility, wind, gusts (warning + danger threshold)
  • Temperature (high + low) and QNH (high + low)
  • Individual enable/disable for each parameter
  • Saved in your browser, automatically restored

SIGMET / AIRMET

Active SIGMETs (hazardous phenomena: thunderstorms, severe icing, turbulence) and AIRMETs (moderate) near your airfield are detected and feed directly into the GO / NO-GO banner.

Icing Level

Evaluates the freezing level (0°C isotherm) and the icing risk if clouds extend above it.

CROSS-COUNTRY NAVIGATION

Local / Navigation Mode

The toggle button LocalNavigation adapts the display:

ModeDisplay
Local LocalReal-time METAR observation, wind/active runway, density altitude, day window, imminent deterioration
Navigation NavigationTAF forecast at arrival time (ETA), viable alternates, en-route weather

When you switch to Navigation mode, the "Departure → Destination" bar appears below the header, the regional map opens and the cursor lands in the Destination field — everything is ready to plan your flight.

Alternates Comparator

In Navigation mode, the tool automatically lists diversion airfields around your destination, with their flight category, visibility, ceiling and wind — sorted by viability for a quick decision.

Departure → Destination Bar

The Destination field accepts an ICAO code (LFPO) or an airfield / city name ("Poitiers"): the list proposes airfields with their distance from your departure, closest first. The selected airfield's name appears under the bar — a safeguard against typos. Changing the destination resets the flight plan: waypoints of the previous route are removed (markers you dropped on the map remain available).

Flight Planner

As soon as a valid destination is entered, the "Flight plan" panel recomputes on every change:

  • Great-circle distance and true/magnetic course (automatic declination)
  • Surface and cruise wind
  • Ground speed and estimated time per leg and cumulated
  • Estimated fuel + legal reserves (day/night VFR)

Waypoints and Markers

Five ways to add a leg:

  • Airfield popup on the map: "+ Waypoint" button;
  • the "+ Waypoint" button in the map toolbar, then click where you want it;
  • right-click anywhere on the map;
  • the plan's "Waypoints" field: ICAO codes or waypoint names, space-separated — VOR, NDB and VFR reporting points added to the plan appear under their real name (e.g. BNE, LOR, E2), technical codes stay hidden;
  • clicking a previously dropped marker → "+ Plan".

Every addition is inserted at the best position: the tool tests all slots and keeps the shortest total route — an intermediate stop automatically slips in before a farther leg. The order of already-entered waypoints is always preserved.

Free markers: a point dropped away from any known airfield defaults to its coordinates (e.g. 4851N 00221W). Name it at creation, or later via the pencil in the leg list or by clicking the marker (Rename). A point dropped near a known airfield simply becomes that leg, named after the airfield.

Managing legs: the plan's leg list shows number, code and name, with a pencil (rename markers) and a cross to remove a leg. On the map, clicking a leg point also offers "Remove from plan" — and "Rename" for markers. Departure, arrival and legs carry their ICAO code as a label (green / red / amber); markers carry their name.

Saving and Importing a Plan

The folder button in the "Departure → Destination" bar offers Save (JSON) — a 100% faithful native format (named markers, altitude, TAS, fuel burn, night flight) — plus GPX export (universal GPS: SkyDemon, Garmin…) and KML (Google Earth), then Import a plan (.json, .gpx, .kml). The save folder is chosen on first use and remembered. On import, any point carrying a known ICAO code becomes that airfield; others become named free markers, and the file's order is preserved.

Weather along the route

This is the regional map (below the airfield's METAR) doing it, automatically: every nearby airfield — including those along your route in Navigation mode — carries a coloured round pin showing its flight category computed from its METAR: green = VFR, blue = marginal VFR, red = IFR conditions, grey = no data. The displayed airfield has a larger amber pin. Click a pin to open that airfield's METAR — handy to spot deteriorating weather toward your next leg. In that panel, the "VAC chart" button opens the airfield's visual approach chart inside the app. And a right-click on a pin adds that airfield to the flight plan as a waypoint (once a destination exists).

Elevation Profile

Samples the terrain elevation profile along the route to verify compliance with VFR clearance rules (500 ft / 1000 ft). Airspaces crossed are shown too: on screen, hover the altitude band to read the sector and its frequency; on the PDF nav log, each crossing is bounded by vertical lines (same blue as the map) with the sector name and frequency written vertically between the bounds.

FREQUENCIES & AIRFIELD INFO

"Frequencies" tab

The airfield's official radio frequencies (official SIA in France, extended worldwide): Tower, Ground, Approach, ATIS, A/A… Each line carries its official remark (approach sectors, auxiliary frequency, covered FIS) and an hours code (H24, HO on request…) explained on hover.

"Airfield info" tab

The airfield sheet: elevation, variation (official, dated), VFR/IFR opening, status, runways at a glance (QFU, true bearing, length × width, surface, threshold elevations) and two collapsible sub-sections: Service hours (AFIS/ATS with the operator's phone) and Fuel (self-service pump, hours, payment).

VAC chart — "Visual approach"

The button at the bottom of the Airfield info tab opens the official VAC chart (official SIA — every French metropolitan airfield, including the smallest) inside the app: zoom, page navigation (recent charts are multi-page) — and every chart you open is stored on your device for offline reading. Airfields without a VAC: link to the country's AIP portal.

COMFORT & CUSTOMIZATION

Dark / light theme

The moon / sun button in the header switches the whole interface between the dark theme (default) and a light theme: texts, widgets, charts and map drawings follow immediately — handy in daylight on the club screen or to your taste.

Express Briefing (Cockpit View)

The button collapses the interface into a single centered column showing only the essentials in large format: GO/NO-GO verdict, flight category, wind/active runway, flight window. Designed for quick reading at 2 m.

Audio Read-Aloud

The button reads the METAR aloud (browser speech synthesis), ATIS-style.

Printable Nav Log

In the "Flight plan" panel, the "Print nav log" button generates the A5 log (3-4 pages: nav log, navigation computations, performance & terrain, weight & balance if the aircraft is configured — beyond 9 legs the nav log continues on a "VFR Flight Log (cont.)" page with the same layout — table filled with blank rows and the Cruise / Turning point / Downwind check boxes kept at the bottom — and the computations table fills the whole page before an extra "Leg details (continued)" page takes over). A verification modal lists the automatically computed values to check before any in-flight use; once confirmed, the PDF opens in a browser tab — the viewer's Print button is one click away (fallback: direct download if the tab is blocked).

Share by Link

The button generates a permalink containing the airfield, raw message and arrival time. A QR code lets you open it on mobile.

Favorites Watchdog

The button activates background monitoring of your favorite airfields. Every 15 min (configurable), the tool recomputes each favorite's flight category and sends a browser notification if a field degrades to CAUTION or NO-GO. The tab must stay open.

METAR DECODING (THE ESSENTIALS)

The application decodes everything automatically — here are simply the keys to read a raw message at a glance.

Annotated example:

LFRV 061230Z 24012KT 9999 FEW035 18/12 Q1021 NOSIG
GroupMeaningKey points
LFRVAirfieldICAO code
061230ZDate-time6th of the month at 12:30 Z (UTC)
24012KTWind240° at 12 kt — 24015G25 = gusts 25 kt, 00000 = calm, VRB = variable
9999Visibilityin metres: 9999 = 10 km or more, 8000 = 8 km… CAVOK = vis ≥ 10 km, no cloud below 5 000 ft, no significant weather
-RA, TS…Present weatherbetween visibility and clouds: -RA light rain, RA rain, TS thunderstorm, BR mist, FG fog, SN snow (most common)
FEW035Cloudsamount + base in hundreds of feet: FEW few, SCT scattered, BKN broken, OVC overcast — FEW035 = base 3 500 ft; NSC = nothing significant
18/12Temp / dew18 °C, dew point 12 °C — a small spread means mist and carb-icing risk
Q1021QNHsea-level pressure: 1021 hPa (Q0998 = below 1000, deterioration likely)
NOSIGTrendno change expected within 2 h; TEMPO = temporary, BECMG = becoming

Rarer codes are in the glossary. The dashboard translates every message: flight category, wind and active runway, minima, trends.

TAF DECODING

General structure

TAF = Terminal Aerodrome Forecast (aerodrome forecast)

TAF LFPG 060500Z 0606/0712 27015KT 9999 SCT040
     BECMG 0609/0611 BKN020
     TEMPO 0615/0618 4000 SHRA BKN012 SCT020CB
     BECMG 0702/0704 34010KT 8000 BKN015

Breakdown :

Element Value Meaning
Type TAF Forecast
ICAO LFPG Airport
Issuance 060500Z Issued on the 06th at 05:00 UTC
Validity 0606/0712 From the 06th at 06h to the 07th at 12h UTC (30h)
Base conditions 27015KT 9999 SCT040 Valid for the entire period except for changes

Change groups

BECMG (BECoMinG) : Permanent change

BECMG 0609/0611 BKN020
│││││ ││││││││ │││││└─ New BKN clouds at 2000 ft
│││││ │││││└└└─ Until day 06 11h UTC
│││││ └└└└────── From day 06 09h UTC
└└└└└────────── Gradual change over this period

The change becomes permanent after the end of the period.

TEMPO (TEMPOrary) : Temporary change

TEMPO 0615/0618 4000 SHRA BKN012
│││││ ││││││││ ││││ ││││ │││││└─ BKN clouds at 1200 ft
│││││ ││││││││ ││││ ││││ └└└└└── Rain showers
│││││ ││││││││ ││││ └└└└──────── Visibility 4000m
│││││ │││││└└└──────────────── Until day 06 18h UTC
│││││ └└└└────────────────────── From day 06 15h UTC
└└└└└────────────────────────── Temporary conditions (< 1h at a time)

Conditions return to normal afterwards.

PROB30/40 : Probability of change

PROB40 0620/0624 2000 +TSRA BKN008CB
│││││││││││││││ ││││ │││││ ││││││└─ CB at 800 ft
│││││││││││││││ ││││ │││││ └└└└└└── Severe thunderstorm with rain
│││││││││││││││ ││││ └└└└──────── Visibility 2000m
│││││││││││││││ └└└└────────────── Until day 06 24h UTC (midnight)
│││││││││││└└└──────────────────── From day 06 20h UTC
└└└└└└────────────────────────── 40% probability

││││││││ ││││││││││ ││││ ││││ │││││└─ BKN clouds at 1000 ft
││││││││ ││││││││││ ││││ ││││ └└└└└── Showers
││││││││ ││││││││││ ││││ └└└└──────── Visibility 3000m

││││││││ └└└└└└─────────────────────── Gusts 35 kt
└└└└└└└└──────────────────────────────── From day 06 at 15:00 UTC sharp

Complete TAF example with commentary

TAF LFPG 060500Z 0606/0712 27015KT 9999 SCT040
     BECMG 0609/0611 BKN020
     TEMPO 0615/0618 4000 SHRA BKN012 SCT020CB
     BECMG 0702/0704 34010KT 8000 BKN015

Translation :

Day 06 06h → Day 06 09h : Wind 270° 15kt, vis ≥10km, scattered clouds 4000 ft
→ Excellent VFR

Day 06 09h → Day 06 11h : Gradual change toward broken clouds 2000 ft
→ Marginal VFR (ceiling lowering)

Day 06 11h → Day 06 15h : BKN clouds 2000 ft (maintained)
→ Marginal VFR

Day 06 15h → Day 06 18h : Temporarily: vis 4000m, showers, BKN 1200 ft, CB 2000 ft
→ IFR (ceiling < 1500 ft + vis < 5km + thunderstorms)

Day 06 18h → Day 07 02h : Return to BKN 2000 ft (no showers)
→ Marginal VFR

Day 07 02h → Day 07 04h : Change: wind 340° 10kt, vis 8km, BKN 1500 ft
→ IFR (ceiling < 1500 ft)

Day 07 04h → Day 07 12h : These conditions maintained
→ IFR

VFR MINIMA (VMC)

VMC conditions in controlled airspace

VMC = Visual Meteorological Conditions (weather conditions for visual flight)

Classes A, B, C, D, E (≥ FL100 / 3000m AMSL)

Parameter Minimum
Visibility ≥ 8 km
Cloud distance (horizontal) ≥ 1500 m
Cloud distance (vertical) ≥ 300 m (1000 ft)

Classes A, B, C, D, E (< FL100 / 3000m AMSL)

Parameter Minimum
Visibility ≥ 5 km
Cloud distance (horizontal) ≥ 1500 m
Cloud distance (vertical) ≥ 300 m (1000 ft)

Class G (< 900m / 3000ft AMSL)

Parameter Minimum
Visibility ≥ 5 km
Cloud distance Clear of clouds and in sight of the ground
Ceiling No regulatory minimum*

*But use common sense: stay high enough to glide in case of engine failure!

Class G (> 900m / 3000ft AMSL)

Parameter Minimum
Visibility ≥ 5 km
Cloud distance (horizontal) ≥ 1500 m
Cloud distance (vertical) ≥ 300 m (1000 ft)

Special VFR (SVFR)

Conditions : - ATC clearance mandatory - In control zone (CTR) only - Visibility ≥ 1500 m (sometimes 800 m at night if qualified) - Clear of clouds - In sight of the ground

Typical use : - Departure/arrival at an aerodrome in degraded weather conditions - Transit through a CTR with weather below VMC

Recommended VFR minimums

Beyond the regulatory minimums, here are the practical thresholds :

Conditions Visibility Ceiling Notes
Comfortable VFR ≥ 10 km ≥ 3000 ft Ideal for navigation
Marginal VFR 5-8 km 1500-3000 ft Navigation possible but stay alert
Limit VFR 5 km 1500 ft Regulatory minimums, little margin
SVFR required 1.5-5 km < 1500 ft ATC clearance mandatory
IFR < 5 km < 1500 ft IFR flight required

Night VFR flight rules

In France (night VFR allowed with qualification) :

Parameter Minimum
Visibility ≥ 5 km
Ceiling ≥ 1500 ft
Qualified pilot Night VFR qualification required

Important : Night VFR is NOT allowed in all European countries!

PRACTICAL EXAMPLES

Example 1 : Excellent VFR (CAVOK)

METAR LFPG 061200Z 27010KT CAVOK 18/08 Q1020 NOSIG

Analysis : - Visibility ≥ 10 km - No clouds < 5000 ft - No significant weather - Light/moderate wind (10 kt) - Stable pressure (1020 hPa - high pressure)

Verdict : EXCELLENT VFR - Ideal for a navigation flight

Example 2 : Marginal VFR (low ceiling)

METAR LFPO 061200Z 34012KT 9999 SCT012 BKN025 11/09 Q1008

Analysis : - Excellent visibility (≥10 km) - Ceiling at 2500 ft (BKN025) - Scattered clouds from 1200 ft - Small T-Td spread (2°C) → high humidity - Falling pressure (1008 hPa)

Verdict : MARGINAL VFR - Local flight OK, navigate with caution

Example 3 : IFR (reduced visibility)

METAR LFRV 061200Z 00000KT 3000 BR SCT004 BKN010 10/09 Q1011

Analysis : - Visibility 3000m (< 5 km) - Ceiling 1000 ft (BKN010) - Scattered clouds from 400 ft - Mist (BR) - Calm wind + T-Td spread 1°C → fog possible

Verdict : IFR - VFR flight prohibited, fly IFR or wait for improvement

Example 4 : Severe IFR (thunderstorm)

METAR LFPO 061200Z 27025G40KT 2000 +TSRA BKN008 SCT015CB 15/13 Q0998

Analysis : - Visibility 2000m (very reduced) - Ceiling 800 ft (very low) - Severe thunderstorm with heavy rain (+TSRA) - Cumulonimbus (CB) - Strong wind with gusts (25G40 kt) - Pressure falling rapidly (998 hPa - low pressure)

Verdict : SEVERE IFR + DANGEROUS CONDITIONS
→ Flight not recommended even under IFR (windshear, turbulence, lightning)

Example 5 : TAF with evolution

TAF LFPG 061200Z 0612/0718 27015KT 9999 SCT040
     BECMG 0615/0617 BKN020
     TEMPO 0618/0624 5000 SHRA BKN012
     BECMG 0703/0705 34010KT CAVOK

Chronological analysis :

Day 06 12h → Day 06 15h : - Wind 270° 15kt - Vis ≥ 10 km - Scattered clouds 4000 ft - EXCELLENT VFR

Day 06 15h → Day 06 17h (BECMG) : - Gradual transition to broken clouds 2000 ft - MARGINAL VFR at the end of the period

Day 06 17h → Day 06 18h : - BKN clouds 2000 ft - MARGINAL VFR

Day 06 18h → Day 07 00h (TEMPO) : - Temporarily: vis 5km, showers, BKN 1200 ft - Temporary IFR (< 1h at a time) - Between showers: return to marginal VFR

Day 07 00h → Day 07 03h : - BKN 2000 ft (without showers) - MARGINAL VFR

Day 07 03h → Day 07 05h (BECMG) : - Gradual transition toward CAVOK - Wind veers to 340° 10kt - EXCELLENT VFR at the end of the period

Day 07 05h → Day 07 18h : - CAVOK maintained - EXCELLENT VFR

Flight decision : - Departure before 15h or after 05h: excellent - 15h-18h: marginal, plan a backup plan - 18h-03h: temporary IFR, VFR flight not recommended

USAGE TIPS

Before the flight

  1. Check several sources :
  2. METAR of the departure aerodrome
  3. METAR of the arrival aerodrome
  4. TAF of both aerodromes
  5. METAR of the alternate aerodromes

  6. Weather charts (TEMSI, WINTEM)

  7. Analyze the evolution :

  8. Compare METAR and TAF
  9. Check the trend (NOSIG, BECMG, TEMPO)

  10. Identify the critical time (IFR/VFR transition)

  11. Calculate your margins :

  12. If conditions close to minimums → plan an alternate
  13. If trend is degrading → depart early or cancel
  14. If TEMPO/PROB → plan arrival outside the critical period

During the flight

  1. Update regularly :
  2. ATIS (Automatic Terminal Information Service)
  3. New METAR every 30 min

  4. Radio contacts with the stations

  5. Be cautious :

  6. If conditions degrade → turn back or divert
  7. Never enter clouds in VFR
  8. Keep a safety margin (fuel, visibility, ceiling)

Chart interpretation

Colored bands : - VFR (Green) : Comfortable flight - MVFR (Blue) : Increased vigilance, plan a backup plan - IFR (Red) : IFR conditions, VFR flight impossible - LIFR (Magenta) : Very poor conditions

Solar curve : - Sunrise/sunset visible on the chart - Plan to avoid flying at dusk (reduced visibility) - Night VFR: check qualification and minimums

Tool limitations

What the tool does : - Displays weather conditions and their time evolution graphically - Automatically parses METAR/TAF and computes the flight category - Synthetic GO / NO-GO verdict crossing all parameters - Computes density altitude (real aircraft performance) - Analyzes QNH pressure trend (deterioration detection) - Displays active SIGMET/AIRMET - Computes the day VFR flight window and alerts on aero night - Regional map: precipitation radar + nearby categories - Alternates comparator and route weather (Navigation mode) - Flight planner: courses, times, fuel, terrain — with waypoints inserted at the shortest route and named markers dropped at the pointer - Generates a printable A5 nav log (opened in a tab, ready to print)

What the tool does NOT do (yet) : - Does not replace the official weather briefing - Does not make decisions for you (the verdict is an aid, not an order) - Does not yet integrate NOTAMs (coming soon) - SIGMET/AIRMET mainly cover the United States for now

The final decision remains that of the pilot in command!
